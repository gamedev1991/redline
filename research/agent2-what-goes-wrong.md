# What Goes Wrong: Clause Types Ranked by Evidence of Harm

Research method: targeted web searches for real complaints, lawsuits, regulatory actions, and legal-aid/journalistic coverage of specific clause types. 8 searches run (within the 12-search cap), 8 distinct sourced findings collected (threshold met, so research stopped there per guardrails). Every item below has a source URL; anything without one was left out.

## 1. Hidden fees / mandatory "junk fees" (leases, rentals, subscriptions)
**Why it hurts people:** Advertised price is not the real price — mandatory fees for things already covered by the base charge get disclosed only after the person has signed or moved in, so they've lost their negotiating leverage and often their ability to walk away without cost.
**Evidence:**
- The DC Attorney General sued Mid-America Apartment Communities (MAA), alleging it charged illegal junk fees and hid the true cost of rent — including "mandatory monthly fees for basic services already included in rent, like common area maintenance," and that "until late 2025, MAA did not disclose in any way the existence of a monthly utility administration fee until a lease had been signed." Source: https://oag.dc.gov/release/attorney-general-schwalb-sues-landlord-charging
- The FTC and Colorado AG sued Greystar (Jan 2025) alleging hidden fees "cost consumers... hundreds of millions of dollars since at least 2019," with consumers "often did not discover the fees until after signing a lease or moving in." Source: https://www.multifamilydive.com/news/ftc-greystar-alleged-fees-rent/737665/

## 2. Auto-renewal / hard-to-cancel subscription clauses
**Why it hurts people:** Contracts renew automatically and companies engineer cancellation to be slow, confusing, or require in-person/certified-mail steps, so people keep getting billed for a service they tried to quit.
**Evidence:**
- FTC's August 2025 lawsuit against LA Fitness alleges it made cancellation "exceedingly hard" by requiring in-person or certified-mail cancellation, "hiding cancellation forms from staff and members," and continuing to bill consumers "even after their banks blocked charges." Source: https://dos.ny.gov/news/consumer-alert-nys-department-states-division-consumer-protection-offers-tips-avoid-costly-mistakes-gym-memberships (search result citing this; primary reporting also at https://www.terms.law/2025/08/25/gym-coaching-and-subscription-scam-demand-letters-getting-out-of-predatory-contracts/)
- A July 2026 California class action against Whoop Inc. alleges the company "signs California customers up for automatically renewing yearly memberships without clear disclosure or consent then bills their cards when the original term ends." Source: https://www.claimdepot.com/cases/whoop-class-action-alleges-auto-renewal-charges-made-without-consumer-consent

## 3. Mandatory arbitration clauses
**Why it hurts people:** By signing, people waive their right to sue, join a class action, or get a jury trial — disputes go to a private arbitrator, often chosen in a process favorable to the company, which sharply reduces the practical odds of recovering damages for fraud, negligence, or defective products/services.
**Evidence:**
- "Forced arbitration clauses are in hundreds of millions of consumer contracts" spanning "car loans and leases, credit cards, checking accounts, insurance, investing accounts, student loans, and even certain employment and nursing home agreements," and consumers "cannot sue for negligence, defective products, or scams" once bound. Source: https://www.americanprogress.org/article/the-case-against-mandatory-consumer-arbitration-clauses/
- The CFPB issued a rule specifically to stop companies from using arbitration clauses "to deny groups of people their day in court," underscoring how widely the clause is used to block collective redress. Source: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/cfpb-issues-rule-ban-companies-using-arbitration-clauses-deny-groups-people-their-day-court/

## 4. Indemnification ("hold harmless") clauses in freelance/contractor agreements
**Why it hurts people:** The freelancer or small business agrees to personally cover the other party's legal costs and damages arising from the work — even when the underlying claim has no merit — which can mean paying attorney's fees and settlements out of pocket, often without the person realizing what they signed up for.
**Evidence:**
- Forbes' contributor agreement required freelance writers to "indemnify Forbes and hold [it] harmless against any liability, cost or expense... incurred as a result of" their work, meaning a freelancer sued over an article could be left covering Forbes' legal costs alone. Source: https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2015/indemnity-clauses-leave-freelancers-open-to-lawsuits/
- "Even if there's no merit to the lawsuit, you might still be required to pay reasonable attorney fees," and without insurance a freelancer "may be on the hook personally for legal fees, damages, and settlements." Source: https://www.forbes.com/sites/artneill/2018/11/14/what-exactly-is-indemnification-and-how-does-it-affect-a-freelance-contributor/

## 5. IP assignment / work-for-hire clauses in freelance contracts
**Why it hurts people:** Freelancers assume "work for hire" language transfers ownership cleanly, but for most freelance categories (design, web dev, illustration, marketing copy) work-for-hire doesn't legally apply unless paired with an explicit assignment clause — leading to ownership disputes over deliverables, especially derivative/future works, that surface only after a falling-out.
**Evidence:**
- "Most freelance work doesn't qualify for work-for-hire protection; logo design, website development, illustrations, photography, and marketing copy are NOT among the nine qualifying categories, and a 'work for hire' clause in these contracts may be legally unenforceable, but signing it still creates disputes and litigation risk." Source: https://clauseshield.app/blog/ip-ownership-clauses-freelancers
- "Failure to explicitly transfer all rights, including future and derivative works, leads to incomplete IP assignment and disputes," and by default "the person who creates the work owns it" absent a proper assignment. Source: https://aaronhall.com/ip-assignment-errors-in-work-for-hire-agreements/

## 6. Non-compete clauses
**Why it hurts people:** Workers — including in low-wage and non-executive roles — get locked out of their own field or geography after leaving a job, and breach can mean being personally sued for large sums, which the FTC found affects roughly one in five US workers.
**Evidence:**
- "Around one in five US workers is bound by a noncompete clause in their current job," and one worker "was sued for $30,000 in 'considerable and irreparable damages' for violating a non-compete." Source: https://www.cnn.com/2024/04/25/success/ftc-noncompete-ban-lawsuit/index.html
- The FTC voted to finalize a rule (April 2024) banning most non-compete clauses in employer-employee contracts, citing the scale of worker harm; the rule was later challenged/blocked in litigation, but the agency continues pursuing enforcement of related harms as of a January 2026 workshop. Source: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/04/ftc-takes-action-against-noncompete-agreements-securing-protections-workers

## 7. "Termination for convenience" / unilateral termination clauses
**Why it hurts people:** One party (usually the customer/general contractor) can end the agreement at any time for no fault-based reason, with little notice — a subcontractor or vendor can be cut off mid-project once a cheaper option appears, and while some compensation for costs-to-date is typical, the abrupt loss of expected future revenue is not.
**Evidence:**
- "This clause is most commonly used either before the subcontractor begins work, or during the project if the general contractor finds a cheaper subcontractor to do the same work," at which point the subcontractor "will typically receive a letter... notifying them of the termination." Source: https://www.levelset.com/blog/termination-for-convenience/
- Courts have had to intervene when a terminating party invoked the clause in bad faith or to disguise a breach of the duty of good faith and fair dealing, indicating this is a recurring point of dispute. Source: https://www.consensusdocs.org/news/think-twice-before-relying-on-a-constructive-termination-for-convenience-clause-after-terminating-a-subcontractor-for-default/

## 8. Liability caps / limitation of liability clauses
**Why it hurts people:** The clause caps what a customer can recover from the vendor (often at "fees paid in the prior 12 months"), so when real damages — e.g., from a data breach — run into the millions, the customer's recovery is capped at a fraction of the actual loss, regardless of fault.
**Evidence:**
- Worked example: "A company signs a SaaS agreement with a $5,000 monthly fee and a limitation of liability that caps damages at 'fees paid in the prior 12 months.' That is $60,000. Then a data breach causes losses in the millions. The limitation of liability clause holds, and the customer is out of luck." Source: https://www.hyperstart.com/blog/limitation-of-liability/
- "Token caps (for example, $100 on a $500,000 deal) face a high risk of being struck down as unreasonable," implying such low, one-sided caps are common enough to be a recognized negotiation problem. Source: https://www.hyperstart.com/blog/limitation-of-liability/
